Predicting UC San Diego admission rates from public school data
Each dot is a California public high school. Its height is the share of the school's UC San Diego applicants who were admitted, pooled over the years shown; its horizontal position is the share predicted by a statistical model that uses only public statistics about the school. Choose the model's variables on the left. By default the model is fitted to schools outside San Diego and Imperial counties and then applied to the schools inside them, which are colored. Click a dot for the school and the arithmetic of its prediction. Scroll or pinch on a chart to zoom; double-click to reset.
Definitions, method, and how to read the numbers

Periods. Entering classes of fall 2015–17, fall 2018–21 and fall 2022–25. A school's admission rate pools the years in its period. The University masks small counts (fewer than 5 applicants or 3 admits); rates use only years in which both the applicant and admit counts are published, and a school is left out when most of its applicants fall in masked years. Schools need at least 30 such applicants in the period.

Fitting. “Linear” is weighted least squares on the admission rate in percentage points. “Logistic” models the log-odds of admission, so predictions stay between 0 and 100%. Both weight each school by its applicants. Variables are standardized before fitting (each is expressed as standard deviations from the applicant-weighted average school in the fitting sample), so coefficients are comparable across variables. Indicator variables (charter, LCFF+, and the San Diego and Imperial County indicators) are left as 0 or 1, so their coefficients are differences between schools with and without the attribute. Linear coefficients are percentage points of admission rate per standard deviation; logistic coefficients are log-odds per standard deviation (near a 25% admission rate, 0.1 in log-odds is about 2 percentage points). Standard errors are in parentheses; for the logistic model they allow for school-level variation beyond binomial chance.

Fit model to. “Schools outside San Diego and Imperial counties” estimates the model without the colored schools and then predicts them, so their actual-minus-predicted values are out-of-sample comparisons with similar schools elsewhere in California. “All schools” includes them; the indicators for San Diego and Imperial County schools can then be added to the model, and their coefficients estimate the same differences directly.

R² and typical miss. R² is the share of applicant-weighted school-to-school variation in admission rates that the model reproduces in the fitting sample; adjusted R² penalizes the number of variables. Typical miss is the applicant-weighted root-mean-square prediction error, in percentage points.

Actual minus predicted. For a group of schools: the group's admits divided by its applicants, minus the applicant-weighted average of the predicted rates. “Admits above prediction” is the difference times the group's applicants. The standard error combines school-level scatter around the model (estimated from the fitting sample) and uncertainty in the fitted coefficients.

Groups. Schools in San Diego and Imperial counties, split into the Sweetwater Union High School District; the San Diego Unified School District; Imperial County; the Poway Unified, San Dieguito Union High and Carlsbad Unified districts; and all other San Diego County public high schools (other districts and charter schools).

Variables. CAASPP: share of grade-11 students meeting the state standard, from the spring before each entering class (no test in spring 2020; spring 2021 omitted for low participation). A–G completion: share of the graduating class completing the course sequence required for UC and CSU eligibility. UPP (unduplicated pupil percentage): share of students who are low-income, English learners or foster youth; the LCFF+ flag marks UPP above 75%. SED and EL: shares of students classified socioeconomically disadvantaged or English learners. School racial composition is from state enrollment counts, grades 9–12 (“Asian” includes Filipino). Neighborhood variables describe the school's census tract (American Community Survey 5-year estimates). Applicant GPA: mean UC-calculated GPA of the school's UC San Diego applicants. GPA curvature adds squared and cubed GPA terms. UC San Diego applicants' GPA relative to all UC applicants: that GPA minus the mean GPA of the school's applicants to any UC campus. Applicants per 100 A–G completers and UC San Diego share of the school's UC applicants describe how deeply a school applies. Admission rate at the other UC campuses: the school's admits divided by applications at the other campuses, same years. Past 6-year graduation: UC graduation of the school's students entering 2004–10. Past yield: share of the school's UC San Diego admits who enrolled, in the five years before the period. Pool-GPA inflation: rise in a school's applicant-pool GPA from 2016 to 2025 beyond what its change in CAASPP results predicts. Distance: straight-line distance to the campus on a log scale (1 = 10 km, 2 = 100 km).

Fitted coefficients

When selected variables are strongly correlated (student poverty and socioeconomic disadvantage correlate at about 0.99), the fit can split one effect between them arbitrarily, even with opposite signs, without changing the predictions. Judge such a variable by what it adds to R², not by its coefficient.
